# Anatomy of a brand guideline

Internal reference for ThinkQuality Studio. Original material, written by the
studio. It records the SHAPE a guideline should take so the generator has a
skeleton; the content of any client guideline comes only from that client's own
data.

## Positioning

Opens the document because everything downstream is judged against it. States
who the brand is for, what it claims, and what it refuses to claim. One or two
sentences, not a paragraph of adjectives. A positioning line that could belong
to a competitor is not a positioning line.

## Audience

Describes the people, not the demographic bracket. What they already believe,
what they are trying to change about themselves, what language they use for
their own problem. Where audience research does not exist, the section says so
rather than inventing a persona — an invented persona gets designed for.

## Voice

The hardest section to write and the most used. Carries real examples of the
brand's own words, not adjectives about them. "Warm and authoritative" tells a
writer nothing; three real sentences the founder actually wrote tell them
everything. Where possible each example carries evidence that it worked.

## Colour

Names each colour, gives its value, and says what it is FOR. A palette without
roles is a swatch library. States which colour dominates, which is reserved for
accent, and which combinations are forbidden. If the palette was derived from
existing work rather than chosen in a studio, the guideline says so — that
provenance is what makes it defensible.

## Typography

Typeface, weights in use, and the rules that keep type consistent: what is set
in the display face, what is body, how headlines are treated. For Arabic brands
this section must address the Arabic face separately from the Latin one; they
are rarely the same family and never behave the same way.

## Logo usage

Placement, minimum size, clear space, permitted colourways, and the misuses
that actually happen — stretched, recoloured, placed on a busy photograph. A
logo section that only shows correct usage does not prevent incorrect usage.

## Social

How the system survives contact with a feed: what a post looks like, how much
text a headline carries, what the first frame of a video must do. Social is
where guidelines break first, because the format punishes anything precious.

## Arabic-specific

Direction is not a styling flag. Covers RTL composition, where the logo sits in
an RTL layout, how Latin fragments and numerals are handled inside Arabic text,
whether kashida elongation is part of the identity, and which Arabic typefaces
are sanctioned. Guidelines written for Latin scripts and translated afterwards
consistently fail here.

## What every section owes the reader

A rule, a reason, and an example. A rule without a reason gets broken the first
time it is inconvenient. A section with no supporting data says so plainly and
names what would fill it.
